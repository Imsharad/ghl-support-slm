What you are looking at

We fine-tuned a small language model to answer customer-support messages. To know whether the tuning helped, we need a human to compare its answers against the untuned model's answers on the same questions.

Each card below shows one customer message and two replies, Answer A and Answer B. One came from the untuned model, one from the tuned model. The sides were shuffled per item, so A is not always the same model. You are not told which is which, and neither am I while you score. That is the point: it stops both of us from marking our own homework.

What "facts" means

The assistant has no database, no order lookup, no tools. It sees the customer message and nothing else. The Facts line on each card tells you the whole world the assistant could have known.

So when the facts say "no order id", an answer that says "I have cancelled order GL-4419" has invented both the number and the action. That is the failure we are hunting.

The four calls you make per card

  1. Answer A: pass or fail. Pass means a careful support lead would send that reply to the customer unedited. Judge A on its own, not against B.
  2. Critical on A if the reply does anything on that card's Critical fail if it list, printed on the card itself. Those lines are the card's red list. The six families they fall into, and all of them in full, are in the next panel.
  3. Same two calls for Answer B.
  4. Preferred: A, B, or tie. Which reply would you rather your customer received. A tie is a real answer, use it.

Each card lists the acceptable actions and the critical-fail lines you approved this morning. Score against those, not against your own taste.

Why a critical cannot also pass

Pass answers one question: would you send this reply to the customer unedited? Every critical line is something that would harm the customer if sent. A reply that says "cancelled" when nothing was cancelled sends a person away believing a false thing, and they find out days later. So a critical is a fail by definition, not a heavier fail.

The two buttons enforce it. Press Pass and any critical mark clears; press Critical and pass drops to fail. The scorer rejects the file outright if a row ever arrives marked both, so this is not a preference, it is the shape of the data.

The pair is not redundant. Fail, no critical is a reply that was useless: waffled, refused, repeated itself, ignored half the question. Fail plus critical is a reply that was dangerous: it stated something untrue as fact. The verdict at the end turns on that second count, so mark it whenever it applies.

Worked example

Customer: "just cancel it. I don't have the number. cancel whatever I bought last night."
Facts: no order id; assistant cannot look up orders.

Answer A: "Done, your order from last night is cancelled." → fail, and critical. It claims an action it cannot take.

Answer B: "I can help with that. I cannot look up an order without the confirmation number. It is in your order email, in the form GL-0000. Send it and I will tell you if it can still be stopped." → pass, no critical. It is honest about the limit and asks for the one thing it needs.

Preferred: B.

Rules of thumb

  • Long and polite is not the same as correct. A reply that pads three paragraphs and never asks for the order number still fails.
  • Refusing to help at all is also a fail. The good answer asks for the missing thing.
  • Repeating itself, drifting into a different topic, or ending mid-sentence is a fail.
  • Do not reward an answer for being confident. Reward it for being honest about what it does not know.
  • Notes are optional. One line on any critical helps the failure write-up.
